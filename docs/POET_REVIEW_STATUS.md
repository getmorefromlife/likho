# Poetry review: what is ready and what we will review together

Updated 29 October 2026. Pronunciation and literary review are deferred at the project owner's request while writing-interface work continues.

## What we have achieved

| Area | What is ready | What this establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pronunciation | A source-attributed dictionary with 40,434 keys and 43,556 readings; 1,007 questionable records are kept separately | We can trace the app's chosen readings to their source. This does not mean every reading has been checked by a poet. |
| Rhythm and metre | One shared analysis path for the editor and companion, with 50 named forms and visible explanations | The app can show how it reached a result and leave unknown words unresolved. Coverage is not universal. |
| Corrections | Suggestions are rescanned against the selected metre; exact fixes and partial improvements have separate labels | A metrical improvement is checked. Meaning, naturalness and literary quality still need a poet's judgment. |
| Personal readings | A review screen allows personal pronunciations to be saved, approved and removed | Personal approval enables local use; it is not independent scholarly certification. |
| Saving and recovery | Draft preservation, truthful save feedback, backup validation, recovery access and active-draft handling | These paths have automated checks. Browser-storage limits still exist. |
| Benchmark preparation | A versioned schema, validator, ten-item Ghalib pilot, frozen source references, blank review worksheets and instructions | The review process can be rehearsed. The ten lines were chosen because the engine accepted them; they cannot establish overall accuracy. |

The ten-item pilot remains `pilot-unadjudicated`; its forms remain `supported: false`. No outside reviewer or adjudicator has validated it. The proposed outreach shortlist and candidate references were not adequately verified and must not be treated as approved recruitment material.

## What you will help us review later

| Review task | Questions we will answer together | What we will record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pronunciation | Is the word read correctly here? Is another pronunciation valid? Are vowels, nasal sounds, aspiration and joined words handled naturally? | Exact word/line, intended reading, alternatives, explanation and source if available |
| Rhythm | Does the line fit the intended بحر? Which syllable or word creates the problem? Is the app applying a licence correctly? | Intended metre, your analysis, app analysis and the point of agreement/disagreement |
| Repairs | Does a suggestion preserve the meaning, grammar, voice and idiom? Is a partial improvement actually useful? | Original line, proposed change, accept/reject/uncertain and your reason |
| Rhymes | Do the words really rhyme under the chosen convention? Is the radif separated correctly? Does the candidate fit the whole line? | Query, pronunciation, candidate, strict/near/not-a-match judgment and contextual fit |
| Missing words | Which familiar words or readings are absent or wrongly quarantined? | Priority, evidence, proposed readings and review decision |
| Ambiguities | Are multiple answers defensible, or is a case disputed? | All defensible alternatives; unresolved cases remain unresolved |

## How our review sessions will work

1. Choose one couplet you know well, or a small group of words.
2. Preserve the exact text, including vowel marks where meaningful.
3. Record your intended reading and judgement, then compare with the app.
4. Classify any disagreement before changing a rule: missing dictionary reading, pronunciation error, contextual-rule error, metre error, bad suggestion or genuine disagreement.
5. Make the smallest justified correction, add positive and negative examples, and re-run the relevant checks.
6. Record your feedback as **poet-led/project-owner review**. Never invent an outside reviewer, signature, review time or independent adjudication.

Your review is useful for development immediately. It remains separate from a future independent evaluation used to support public accuracy or competitor-superiority claims.
